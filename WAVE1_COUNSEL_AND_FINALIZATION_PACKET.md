## 4. Questions for qualified counsel / claims reviewers

### Every SKU

1. Is the controlled title/subtitle/cover/listing language supportable as personal reflection/workbook language within the intended territory and channel?
2. Does any page, image, metadata field, category, keyword, prompt, disclaimer, or back-cover copy create a treatment, outcome, diagnosis, professional-advice, or regulatory implication that should be removed or qualified?
3. Does the planned author/imprint/collection presentation create an avoidable clearance, false-affiliation, or consumer-confusion concern?
4. Are there required disclosures, age/audience limits, disclaimers, or review steps that must apply to a particular product surface?
5. Is any specific phrase unacceptable in a future Spanish, French, Hindi, Simplified Chinese, Hausa, or Yorùbá paired edition without localized legal/claims review?

### A01 — GLP-1 companion

- Confirm whether “GLP-1” is acceptable in title, subtitle, cover, listing, and keywords in the intended consumer/reflection context.
- Identify any needed distance from dosing, injection, side-effect, efficacy, nutrition, body/weight, or named-drug language.
- Confirm whether the existing care-team and emergency language is sufficient, excessive, or needs a jurisdiction-specific adjustment.

### A04 — self-talk diary

- Confirm the product is not implicitly marketed as therapy, trauma care, or treatment of a mental-health condition.
- Identify any self-talk, gratitude, or affirmation wording that could imply a therapeutic outcome.

### A05 — sleep reflection companion

- Confirm all sleep/wind-down language avoids insomnia relief, treatment, medication, or restorative-sleep outcome claims.
- Identify any support/escalation wording required for difficult nights or safety-sensitive content.

### B10 — breath-paced planner

- Confirm breath invitations, adaptation language, and “regulate” wording do not imply a physiological outcome, treatment, vagus stimulation, or required breathing protocol.
- Identify any phrase that should be revised for panic, respiratory discomfort, disability/access, or safety context.

### B12 — productivity rescue workbook

- Confirm the “burnout” and “rescue” vocabulary remains within reflection/planning support and does not create treatment, employment, or workplace-legal advice implications.
- Identify any resource or boundary statement required where low capacity, distress, or work harm is discussed.

### B18 — financial-anxiety workbook

- Confirm all financial content remains neutral reflection rather than financial, investment, legal, tax, credit, or mental-health advice.
- Identify any wording that could be read as a financial outcome, savings/debt result, or anxiety-treatment promise.
