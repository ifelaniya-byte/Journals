# Pocket of Calm — 54 final deck prompts

## ARRIVE
Notice your body and surroundings.

1. Name three things in this room that make you feel a little more here.
2. Let your eyes land on one ordinary object. What do you notice about it?
3. Where is your body being supported right now?
4. What sound is closest? What sound is farthest away?
5. If you could give this moment a weather report, what would it be?
6. Notice your hands. What are they doing, holding, or asking for?
7. Name one thing that is true in this exact minute.
8. What changes when you lengthen only the exhale, naturally and without force?
9. What is one place you could make two inches more comfortable?

## SOFTEN
Practice a more humane sentence with yourself.

1. What is one sentence you would rather hear than the one your inner critic is offering?
2. What would “kind enough” sound like here?
3. What would you say to someone you love in this exact moment?
4. What are you allowed to be learning?
5. Which expectation could become a request instead?
6. What deserves less judgment today?
7. What can be true without being fixed tonight?
8. What is one thing you did that was quietly difficult?
9. What would it mean to take your own side for five minutes?

## NOURISH
Become curious about comfort and care.

1. What kind of comfort sounds possible, not perfect?
2. What sensory cue might help: warmth, water, air, texture, quiet, or movement?
3. What would make the next hour five percent gentler?
4. What have you been needing but postponing?
5. What is one ordinary thing that helps you feel more like yourself?
6. If care were practical today, what would it look like?
7. What would a good-enough pause include?
8. What do you want less of around you right now?
9. What is one small pleasure you do not have to earn?

## REACH
Name the support or boundary you need.

1. What kind of support would make this lighter to carry?
2. Who could receive one honest sentence from you?
3. What boundary would protect your next hour?
4. What question could you ask instead of trying to guess?
5. What task is not yours to solve alone?
6. What would a clear “not today” make room for?
7. Where could you ask for help before you are at capacity?
8. What conversation needs a softer beginning?
9. What do you need someone else to understand about today?

## CONTINUE
Recognize what is already working.

1. What has been helping, even a little?
2. What did you do today that you almost failed to count?
3. Where did you choose a sustainable pace?
4. What is one thing you have already survived or learned?
5. What would make progress feel less like a scoreboard?
6. What is worth repeating tomorrow?
7. What part of your routine is quietly working?
8. What did you notice with curiosity instead of judgment?
9. What is one reason to trust your ability to continue?

## RESET
Find one small next step on a hard day.

1. If today needed to be smaller, what could become optional?
2. What is the next physical action—not the whole project?
3. What can wait until you have more information or energy?
4. What can you set down for ten minutes?
5. What is one kind way to end this loop?
6. What needs a list, a timer, a glass of water, or a message?
7. What would you like the next hour to feel like?
8. What is one decision you do not have to make right now?
9. What would “enough for today” look like?
